Lesson 0002 • Architecture & Workflow

From Email Chaos to a Structured Pipeline

Solving the Google Doc version scramble, advisor black hole, and alumni scavenger hunt.

In your audio note, you pinpointed the exact triad of headaches that every medical student and residency advisor endures:

  • Google Doc version sprawl: Juggling separate files (Draft_v1, v2_edits, v3_final), losing track of comments, and worrying about formatting breaking when copy-pasting into ERAS come June/July.
  • The communication black hole: Emailing busy faculty advisors and wondering: Did they open it? Are they reviewing it? When will I hear back?
  • The alumni scavenger hunt: Trying to figure out which past graduates matched at target programs and hunting down their contact info across scattered spreadsheets.

These aren’t just personal frustrations—they are classic information architecture failures. Let's see how a web application fixes all three using two foundational software patterns: State Machines and Relational Connections.

Solution 1: The Document State Machine

Instead of sending an untracked Word or Google Doc over email, a web application gives every essay an explicit lifecycle status. The system itself acts as the traffic controller:

Solution 2: Relational Tables End the "Alumni Scavenger Hunt"

In a spreadsheet, if you want to know who matched at a hospital, someone has to manually search through rows or ask alumni affairs. In a web app's relational database, the data is already linked like puzzle pieces:

The Three Connected Tables

1. ACGME Directory: Contains verified program IDs, names, cities, and specialties.
2. Match History Table: Records which senior from your school matched into which program ID in what year.
3. Alumni Directory: Records current contact info, willingness to mentor, and advice notes.

When a student adds a program to their school list, the database automatically queries: "Fetch all alumni where ProgramID matches this hospital." The student gets instant contact cards without anyone searching manually.
